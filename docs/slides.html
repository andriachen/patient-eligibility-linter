Live demo · the Linter, running Claude Haiku 5.504 / 09

Our user writes the protocol.

  1. Paste inclusion criteria into one box and exclusion criteria into another.
  2. Claude reads them with the same prompt that extracted all 1,011 trials. Every flag is verified to quote the draft word for word before it is shown.
  3. Each flag carries a suggested rewording and names the FDA or ASCO–Friends guidance behind it, in three tiers: fix first, worth a look, team’s call.
  4. “In the draft” highlights every flag in place; “Copy report” hands a review-ready note to the team.
  5. The Map tab shows where each rule bites, county by county, with CDC PLACES and MassGIS EJ context.

The tool suggests. The protocol team decides.

Composed draft · Claude Haiku 5.5 · 8 of 13 categories flagged · more than 83% of 1,011 MA trials restrict fewer
Inclusion Criteria:
* Age 18 to 75 years
* ECOG performance status 0-1
* Creatinine clearance >= 60 mL/min
* Must be able to read and speak English

Exclusion Criteria:
* Known HIV infection
* Brain metastases, treated or untreated
* Any prior malignancy within 5 years
* Pregnant or breastfeeding

How we earn trust07 / 09

Every flag shows its receipt

The live Linter uses the same Claude model and prompt that extracted the 1,011-trial dataset. Every flag is checked against the draft word for word before it is shown; the Linter won’t return a quote it can’t verify.

What our numbers don’t say08 / 09
County, not person

CDC PLACES and MassGIS figures are county or neighborhood estimates. We never add or multiply them across criteria.

Sites, not catchments

We place a trial in the county of its site. Patients travel, so this is a simplification.

EJ areas only

Limited-English figures cover environmental-justice block groups, not the whole county.

Small categories

Diabetes, BMI and smoking have two labeled examples each. We report them, but we don’t score them.

Guardrails from our project plan, applied on every screen.
Why it matters09 / 09

Narrow criteria slow enrollment and decide who gets in. Fixing one rule helps both.

Massachusetts is great at discovery. We fix the step where discovery becomes a trial.
